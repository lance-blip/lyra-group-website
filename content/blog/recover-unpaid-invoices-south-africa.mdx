## Why invoices go unpaid (even when the work was real)

Not every non-payment is fraud. Understanding the pattern helps you respond with the right intensity.

**Cash-flow stress at the debtor.** Many SA businesses pay when they get paid. Your invoice can be “approved” and still sit in a queue behind salaries, SARS, and louder suppliers.

**Admin friction.** Wrong PO numbers, missing goods-received notes, invoices sent to a generic inbox, or disputes about partial delivery create delay that looks like refusal.

**Relationship avoidance.** Especially in B2B networks, debtors stall because confrontation feels costly. Silence is cheaper than a hard conversation — until you make silence expensive.

**Process failure on your side.** Weak credit checks, no deposits on high-risk jobs, verbal variations never reduced to writing, and no escalation ladder all turn one late payment into a portfolio problem.

**Strategic non-payment.** A minority of debtors push every supplier to the edge on purpose. They pay the squeaky wheel last — or never.

Your job is not to diagnose character from day one. Your job is to run a repeatable recovery process that separates temporary delay from permanent leakage.

## The real cost of “we’ll wait a bit longer”

Owners often underestimate soft costs:

- **Working-capital strain** — you pay staff, fuel, stock, and VAT cycles while receivables age.
- **Opportunity cost** — money stuck in old invoices cannot fund growth, marketing, or a buffer for load-shedding and logistics shocks.
- **Team burnout** — your best people become part-time collectors instead of operators.
- **Prescription risk** — debts can become harder (or impossible) to enforce if left too long. South African prescription rules are technical; do not assume endless time. When in doubt, get advice early rather than after the window narrows.
- **Evidence decay** — staff leave, emails archive, delivery drivers forget details. Old files are weaker files.

Waiting is a strategy only when it is deliberate, time-boxed, and documented.

## Internal recovery: do this before you outsource

Professional collection works best when you have already been clear, fair, and organised. Run this internal ladder first.

### 1) Confirm the debt is due and liquid

Gather:

- Signed quote, contract, or terms of trade  
- Purchase order (if required by the customer)  
- Proof of delivery / job completion / acceptance  
- Tax invoice and statement of account  
- Payment terms and any agreed variations  
- Prior payment history and contact persons  

If the amount is genuinely disputed on quality or scope, collection pressure alone will not magic agreement into existence. Fix the factual baseline first.

### 2) Make contact like a professional, not a prosecutor

Within days of due date:

- Email the accounts contact and the relationship owner  
- Call once, note the date, person, and commitment  
- Confirm any promise-to-pay in writing  

Tone matters. You are protecting cash flow, not performing anger. South African business culture still runs on relationships — firm and respectful beats theatrical.

### 3) Send a structured reminder sequence

A simple sequence many SMEs use:

| Stage | Timing (example) | Action |
|------|-------------------|--------|
| Soft reminder | 1–7 days overdue | Friendly email + statement |
| Firm reminder | 8–21 days | Call + email naming amount, invoice numbers, due date |
| Final internal notice | 22–35 days | Written notice that formal demand / external recovery is next |
| Formal demand | After internal fails | Letter of demand with deadline |
| External recovery | After demand ignored | Registered collector / legal pathway |

Adjust timing to your industry. Construction retentions and large wholesale accounts may need tailored terms — but “no process” is not a strategy.

### 4) Issue a proper letter of demand

A **letter of demand** in South Africa is a formal written notice that:

- Identifies creditor and debtor  
- States the amount and what it relates to  
- References invoices / agreements  
- Sets a clear deadline to pay or make arrangements  
- Reserves rights if payment is not made  

Send it in a way you can prove (email with read markers where appropriate, and/or registered post / courier where the relationship or amount justifies it). Keep proof of service with the file.

A demand letter is often the moment “we’ll pay next week” becomes a real conversation.

### 5) Decide: settle, structure, or escalate

Outcomes after demand:

- **Full payment**  
- **Acknowledgement + payment plan** (get it in writing; consider partial upfront)  
- **Genuine dispute** (evidence exchange; possible negotiation or legal advice)  
- **Silence / bad faith** → external recovery  

If you reach silence or bad faith, continuing only with polite internal emails usually trains the debtor that you are optional.

## When to use a professional debt collector

Bring in a professional when:

- Internal reminders and a formal demand failed  
- The balance is material to cash flow  
- Your team lacks time, skill, or emotional distance  
- Multiple accounts are ageing at once  
- The debtor is responsive only to external pressure  
- You need POPIA-aware, legislation-aware engagement at scale  

You do **not** need to wait until the business is in crisis. The best time to mandate a collector is while the debt is still commercially “alive.”

### What professional B2B recovery actually looks like

Reputable commercial collection is not random harassment. A typical path:

1. **Mandate & file audit** — you authorise pursuit; documents are checked.  
2. **Debtor engagement** — professional contact across channels, with records.  
3. **Negotiation** — lump sum, timed plan, or settlement where commercial.  
4. **Escalation options** — stronger formal steps or legal handoff if needed.  
5. **Remittance** — recovered funds paid over per mandate, less agreed commission.

## What to look for in a debt collection agency (SA checklist)

Use this checklist before you sign anything:

**1. Compliance posture you can verify**  
Ask how they approach the Debt Collectors Act framework, Council for Debt Collectors expectations where applicable, POPIA, and lawful communication practices. Compliance is not their vanity metric — it is your brand risk shield.

**2. Commercial vs consumer competence**  
B2B recovery is not identical to consumer book-running. If your pain is unpaid trade invoices, choose a team that lives in commercial files. (See our companion guide on commercial vs consumer collection.)

**3. Fee clarity — especially “No Collection, No Fee”**  
Commission-only models align incentives, but read the mandate for:

- Commission percentage and trigger  
- What counts as a “collection” (full vs payment plan)  
- Disbursements  
- Legal cost authorisation rules  
- Withdrawal / dual-mandate terms  

**4. Reporting and human access**  
You should know status without chasing the chaser. You should also get a human when a matter is sensitive.

**5. Reputation and tone**  
Your debtor may still be a customer, a supplier elsewhere, or part of a small industry network. Brutal short-term tactics can create long-term commercial damage.

## Practical document pack to hand over

Speed up recovery by preparing:

- Company details (creditor) and debtor legal name / registration if known  
- Age analysis (debtor statement)  
- Invoices and proof of delivery/performance  
- Contracts and T&Cs  
- Prior correspondence and call notes  
- Any dispute history and your position  
- Known contacts, phone numbers, emails, addresses  
- Interest / costs position if contractually provided (don’t invent numbers)

Clean files collect faster. Messy files collect excuses.

## Timelines: how long does debt collection take?

There is no honest single number. Time depends on:

- Age of debt  
- Debtor solvency and willingness  
- Quality of documents  
- Dispute complexity  
- Whether litigation becomes necessary  

**Early-stage commercial files** can resolve in weeks when the debtor has means and just needed structured pressure.  
**Hard files** can take months, especially if legal process is required.  
**Insolvent or disappeared debtors** may yield partial recoveries — or none.

A good agency will not promise magic dates. They will promise process, communication, and lawful persistence.

## Common SME mistakes that kill recoveries

- **Invoicing the wrong entity** (trading name vs legal entity)  
- **No written terms** on credit  
- **Continuing to supply** a non-paying account without cash-upfront rules  
- **Verbal settlements** never confirmed  
- **Emotional calls** that create unnecessary conflict or admissions risk  
- **Dual-instructing multiple collectors** without coordination  
- **Waiting a year** then expecting easy results  
- **Choosing the cheapest agency** with the roughest methods  

Recovery is a systems problem. Treat it like one.
